How it works

  1. Scan. Every weekday a script checks the Federal Register (where US rules are published) and recent news for terms like semiconductor, advanced computing, gallium, germanium, rare earth, tungsten, and Section 232.
  2. Tag. An LLM (Claude) reads each item and fills a fixed form: rule type, status, dates, countries, which stages of the map it hits, key numbers such as a tariff rate, a summary, and a confidence level. Without an API key, a simple keyword tagger runs instead.
  3. Review. New items arrive as pending. A person approves them before they affect the map or the cost model, because an LLM can misread a rule.
  4. Model. The cost model is plain code, not the LLM. It splits the GPU's manufacturing cost into HBM, logic die, packaging, substrate, and other; applies the scenario's price changes; passes a chosen share to the buyer; then adds any tariff on the import value.

The cost formula

Manufacturing cost = price × (1 − gross margin). Each component's cost = manufacturing cost × its share. Mineral shocks reach the GPU through logic die and HBM inputs, assumed at 3% of their cost. New price = price + (sum of component cost changes × pass through) + tariff × that price, if the tariff applies.

Key assumptions to replace during review

  • GPU prices are rough public estimates (NVIDIA said in 2024 a Blackwell GPU would cost $30,000 to $40,000). The MI325X price is a placeholder.
  • Gross margin default uses NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 GAAP gross margin of 71.1%.
  • The cost split (HBM 50%, logic die 18%, packaging 17%, substrate 5%, other 10%) is an illustrative starting point, not a sourced figure.
  • The tariff is applied to the GPU price as a stand in for its customs value.
